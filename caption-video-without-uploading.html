How to add captions to a video without uploading it

Almost every caption service works the same way. You upload your video to their website, their computers listen to it, and you download the captions. It is convenient, and most of the time it is fine.

Sometimes it is not fine. The video might be client work you are not allowed to share. It might be an interview that has not been published yet, a family recording, or footage a lawyer or journalist would rather keep off a stranger's server. Uploading also means waiting for a big file to transfer, and it usually means a subscription, because the services charge by the month or by the minute of video.

There is another way to do it. A program that runs on your own computer can listen to the speech and write the captions itself. Nothing is sent anywhere, because there is nowhere for it to go. What comes out is a caption file, ending in .srt or .vtt, which are the two formats almost everything accepts.

A caption file is separate from the video itself. You keep your video exactly as it is, and the caption file sits alongside it. Video editors will load it onto your timeline. YouTube and Vimeo let you upload it next to a video you publish. Most video players will show the captions if the file is in the same folder with the same name. So the captions can be made in private, on your machine, and only used wherever you decide to put them.

Doing it with CaptionTool

CaptionTool is the program we make for this. Here is the whole process, start to finish.

  1. Open CaptionTool and drop in your video or audio file. It runs on Windows, Mac and Linux.
  2. Choose how it should listen. Accurate is slower and makes the best captions it can. Fast draft is quicker, for when you want something rough to tidy up.
  3. Wait while it works. The file never leaves your computer. The program has no network code at all, so it could not send your footage anywhere even if it wanted to.
  4. When it finishes, you get two caption files, one .srt and one .vtt, saved next to your video.
  5. Add the caption file wherever the video is going: your editor, the upload page of the site you publish on, or the folder the player reads from.

What to expect from the captions

On a clear recording of one person talking, the captions come out close to the words that were said, and you may only need to fix the odd name or place. Busier recordings, with music, people talking over each other or strong background noise, will need more tidying by hand.

Two honest limits. The current version only understands English. And when someone talks very fast, a few of their captions will run quicker than is comfortable to read, because the words are shown exactly as they were spoken rather than cut down to fit.

If your videos are fine to upload, the subscription services will suit you well. If they are not, this is the way to caption them anyway, with the footage staying where it belongs.
